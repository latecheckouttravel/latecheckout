A hotel-first guide to Kauai, Maui, Oahu, Lanai and Hawaii Island.

Find your fit

What each Hawaiian island does best

Choosing the island first makes every hotel decision easier. Here is the short version before you go deeper.

01

Kauai

Best for dramatic nature, quiet and a slower pace.

The Garden Isle is Hawaii at its greenest: the Na Pali Coast, deep valleys, waterfalls and beaches framed by mountains. Kauai suits travelers who would rather hike, kayak or watch the weather move across Hanalei Bay than build a packed itinerary. The island feels peaceful and relatively unhurried, with luxury stays concentrated around the North Shore and sunny Poipu.

02

Maui

Best for a polished beach vacation with plenty beyond the resort.

Maui is the great all-rounder. It combines strong luxury hotels, golden beaches, destination restaurants and memorable day trips, from Haleakala to the Road to Hana. Wailea is especially easy for couples and families who want beautiful resorts and dependable sunshine without giving up opportunities to explore.

03

Oahu

Best for first-time visitors, food, culture and variety.

Oahu pairs urban Honolulu and Waikiki with the surf culture and open landscapes of the North Shore. It has the broadest mix of hotels, restaurants, history, shopping and nightlife—and the easiest air access. Choose Oahu when you want beach time but do not want the entire vacation to happen inside a resort.

04

Lanai

Best for seclusion, privacy and doing very little beautifully.

Small and quiet, Lanai is the most resort-centered choice on this list. It works for honeymoons, milestone trips and travelers who want to disappear for a few days. The appeal is not a long sightseeing checklist; it is space, personal service and the feeling of being removed from everything.

05

Hawaii Island (the Big Island)

Best for volcanic landscapes, adventure and exceptional luxury resorts.

Hawaii Island has the greatest sense of scale and contrast: lava fields, rainforest, black-sand beaches, high summits and the sunny Kona and Kohala coasts. It is ideal when you want both a resort worth staying in and a landscape that feels genuinely different. Driving distances are longer here, so choosing the right hotel and location matters.

A closer look

Hawaii Island, hotel by hotel

The Island of Hawaii rewards travelers who pick their resort deliberately. Along the Kona and Kohala coasts, three hotels stand out—but they create three very different vacations.

01

Four Seasons Resort Hualalai

Best for The most complete luxury resort experience.

Hualalai is the polished all-rounder: oceanfront rooms and villas, multiple pools, golf, strong dining and enough to do that the resort can be the destination. It is an easy first choice for families and multigenerational trips that want range without sacrificing service.

02

Kona Village, A Rosewood Resort

Best for Privacy, place and a slower rhythm.

Freestanding thatched-roof hale and open-air living give Kona Village a rare sense of seclusion. Choose it when the dream is barefoot, quiet and deeply connected to the landscape—not a conventional resort tower.

03

Mauna Lani, Auberge Collection

Best for Relaxed Kohala Coast luxury.

Mauna Lani balances a beautiful swimmable coastline, wellness, cultural programming and a social but easygoing resort atmosphere. It suits couples and families who want thoughtful design with room to explore.

The short answer

Which Big Island hotel is right for you?

Choose Hualalai when you want the broadest resort experience, superb service and something for every generation.

Choose Kona Village when privacy, standalone accommodations and a strong sense of place matter most.

Choose Mauna Lani when you want relaxed modern luxury, an excellent beach and a well-rounded Kohala Coast base.

Good to know

Hawaii hotel planning FAQs

Which Hawaiian island is best for a first-time visit?

Oahu is often the easiest first visit because it combines beaches, food, culture, Honolulu and the North Shore with the widest range of flights and hotels. Maui is a strong alternative for travelers who want a more resort-led vacation.

Which Hawaiian island has the best luxury hotels?

Hawaii Island, Maui and Lanai have especially strong luxury resort options. Hawaii Island stands out for Four Seasons Resort Hualalai, Kona Village, A Rosewood Resort and Mauna Lani, Auberge Collection.

Should I visit one Hawaiian island or two?

For a trip of about a week, one island usually creates a more relaxed vacation. With ten nights or more, two islands can work well if their experiences are meaningfully different and the inter-island travel time is worthwhile.
